**TAPE — an undeletable call record on ZeroClaw + Solana**

Every crypto caller has a great record, because they delete the bad calls. TAPE is one they can't — the timestamp isn't ours, it's Solana's.

A ZeroClaw agent **records** calls on a real Telegram channel, commits each into a Merkle root, and anchors it to Solana. **It places no trades** — no orders, no funds, no venue write API; it makes callers accountable for calls they make elsewhere. **The video is a phone recording of exactly that**: he asks what he can call, the agent lists markets, he replies "3 no 0.5050", it comes back anchored with a Solscan link — slot 437624124. One rule: **post a position and it stays open and marks to market until you publicly close it.** A 90% win rate with one −$400k open bag ranks below 55% with clean books.

**Live on mainnet, unattended, over a week — not built for the video.** 2,000+ anchors across 9+ unbroken days, all mainnet. `node bin/anchor-stats.js` prints the live figure *and* our three outages (61, 87, 27 min) rather than smoothing them — the 87-min one was the daemon dying silently with nothing noticing, which is why it has a health canary now.

**The part I'd like you to attack: the verifier can convict us.** It shares no code with what writes the records, and talks to Solana, not our server. In the video I edit one number in that receipt (0.5050 → 0.4200) and run it: our own tool prints `FRAUD`, exit 1. A verifier that can only ever confirm is a logo.

**Fund safety, tested two ways.** Against the **live agent on the real payer**: a call whose note impersonates me and orders the anchor wallet swept to an attacker first. Balance read from Solana either side — **delta 0 lamports**. And against a *fully compromised* model, the durable claim: the anchor key **cannot express** a transfer. `lib/signing-policy.js` certifies the serialized tx against intent (`{ComputeBudget, Memo}`, payer-only) before the signer is reached.
