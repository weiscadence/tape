**TAPE — an undeletable call record on ZeroClaw + Solana**

Every crypto caller has a great track record, because they delete the bad calls. TAPE is one they can't delete. The timestamp isn't ours, it's Solana's.

A ZeroClaw agent takes calls on a real Telegram channel, commits each into a Merkle root, and anchors that root to Solana mainnet. **The video is a phone recording of exactly that**: he asks what he can call, the agent lists live markets, he replies "3 no 0.5050", and it comes back anchored with a Solscan link — seq 367, slot 437624124. One rule: **post a position and it stays open and marks to market until you publicly close it.** No delete, no edit, no quiet abandonment. A 90% win rate with one −$400k open bag ranks below 55% with clean books.

**Live on mainnet, unattended, for over a week — not built for the video.** 1,700+ anchors across 8+ unbroken days, all mainnet, real lamports. `node bin/anchor-stats.js` prints the live figure, and our three outages (61, 87, 27 min) rather than smoothing them — the 87-minute one was the daemon dying silently with nothing noticing, which is why it has a health canary now.

**The part I'd like you to attack: the verifier can convict us.** It shares no code with the thing that writes the records, and talks to Solana, not our server. In the video I take the receipt for the call he just made, edit one number (0.5050 → 0.4200) and run it: our own tool prints `FRAUD`, exit 1. A verifier that can only ever confirm is a logo.

**Fund safety.** Our injection test assumes the attack already won — the model is compromised, assembling the attacker's transfer. It still fails, because the anchor key *cannot express* a transfer: `lib/signing-policy.js` is a deny-by-default gate certifying the serialized tx against intent (`{ComputeBudget, Memo}`, payer-only) before the signer is reached. So "refused" is distinguishable from "sent and failed". 3/3, fails closed.
